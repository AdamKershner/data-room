## Cross-cutting themes

These are the handful of things that repeat. Fix them once as platform / writing rules and most of the SOP-specific notes get cheaper.

### 1. Collapse the one-line page layer

If a SOP has ~10 subheadings and each one opens 10–12 pages of one or two sentences, drop that bottom layer. Keep the subheadings. Clicking a subheading should open **one page** with all the lines already on it, checkbox next to each.

Nothing needs rewriting. SOP 1 would be ~3 pages instead of 31. SOP 4 would be ~10 instead of ~100. Clicking through endless line items is fatigue-inducing.

### 2. One job, one heading

Do not split one job across consecutive pages that restate the same sequence. If the intro already says it, do not also book it, confirm it, and run it as three separate pages.

Same for two-audience versions of one task (e.g. testimonials from teammates vs community). One heading, responsibilities listed underneath.

### 3. “Done when” is repeating the title

The green Done when box is usually the heading again: a page titled “Request access” with “Done when: Request access.” Delete the box wherever it restates the heading.

Twice it is broken because something prefixes **Confirm** onto the title:

- SOP 12: “Confirm type the seed into Google and YouTube search”
- SOP 21: “Confirm clarify the decision and the deadline”

Neither is a sentence. The auto-prefix should not exist, or it should only fire when the heading is not already an action.

### 4. Rules are not tasks

Standing rules (“do not…”) should not have a checkbox, a time estimate, and a Done when that repeats the rule. You cannot complete something that cannot be done. Either:

- pull every “do not” into a **rules block at the top**, and leave only expected actions in the checklist, or
- if it must stay in the list, Done when = the person has **read** it.

Otherwise someone ticks eight boxes and thinks PII is handled.

### 5. Pointer pages and example-as-instruction

A whole page whose only content is “use SOP X instead” is wasted. Inline the pointer or deep-link to the section.

Examples written as permanent steps (e.g. “Link Amy Wang’s YouTube as @wamyy5”) read as instructions. Label them as examples.

### 6. Links, deep-links, and footer navigation

- Links to other SOPs should land on the relevant **section**, not the top of the whole SOP. Even better: “refer to [named section] of SOP N” plus the link.
- Consecutive SOPs should have footer next/prev links. SOP 3 was missing a footer to SOP 4; the others had them.
- Vendor homepages are the wrong target when we have a store, brand folder, or channel. Figma was not linked at all.
- Broken / unavailable destinations (YouTube @kahanaHQ) need a visibility or indexing check.

### 7. Multiple jobs in one SOP

If a SOP clearly contains two or three different jobs (who owns what vs 90-day ramp vs general practices; form-filler vs follow-up; daily checklist vs incident), split by job, not arbitrarily in half.

### 8. Cadence vs click-through format

Daily SOPs and incident SOPs should not be seven-page click-through checklists. Nobody is doing that every morning, and nobody is doing it while something is actively going wrong.

## SOP-by-SOP

### SOP 1 — Product Hunt Launch

Repetitive; shorten instead of restating the same sequence.

**Town halls** are the clearest example: three separate pages (book three consecutive ones, confirm everyone signed up, run all three) plus an intro paragraph that already says all of that. Said four times before anyone has done anything.

**Testimonials** are the same job split by audience: teammates on one page, community supporters on another, the 40 tally on a third. One heading, two audiences, responsibilities listed underneath.

Flattened, this SOP would be **3 pages instead of 31**.

### SOP 2 — Community Building

Subheadings and content felt concise and digestible. Some of it is just walking someone through the UI (open the Members tab, invite by email, copy and share the join link) and felt intuitive.

### SOP 3 — Product Quality

Structure is different from 1 and 2. Earlier SOPs open a subheading onto a line or two; here it is one or two paragraphs of instructions. Flagged in case there is a intended house style.

**Deep-link:** under “Mine feedback you already have,” the community building (logging) link opens all of SOP 2 instead of the logging-feedback section. Auto-scroll to that section, or write “refer to Logging feedback and issues in SOP 2” plus the link.

**Footer:** could not go SOP 3 → SOP 4. No next-SOP footer. Had to go back to the gallery. Did not happen on the other SOPs.

### SOP 4 — Product Management Playbook

Overcrowded. Too many sections and subsections; scrolling to the bottom takes a while. Flattened, about **10 pages instead of ~100**.

Do not split in half. There are **three jobs** in one SOP:

1. Who owns what across functions
2. A 90-day ramp for one new PM
3. General practices (e.g. writing before meetings)

### SOP 5 — Blogs

Some of it felt intuitive and did not need steps (may be because she has used the platform).

**Broken link:** “Publish on the Kahana HQ” → YouTube `@kahanaHQ` — page is not available. Suspect visibility or indexing, not a wrong URL.

### SOP 6 — Brand Guidelines

The six personality notes are never explained and have no link out. “Killer Cuteness” is not something you can act on. A couple of examples each would fix it.

**Title truncation:** four titles cut off mid-sentence, e.g. “…Exhilarating Exploration, Killer” and “Never forget who creators”.

### SOP 7 — Merch

Canva and Printify are mentioned but the links go to vendor homepages, not our store and brand folder. Figma is not linked at all.

### SOP 8 — Official Social Media

First place the green Done when box showed up as useless. Page “Request access” → “Done when: Request access.” This pattern is **across all 30**. Delete the box anywhere it repeats the heading.

### SOP 9 — Author Outreach

Opening the contact list and opening the tracker are two pages for opening two tabs of the same sheet.

### SOP 10 — Writing a Project Charter

Should be a **template**, not 19 pages. The output is one document, so one page showing the charter with each field explained under it.

### SOP 11 — Creating YouTube Videos

Long; some content could be cut. Keep the **description template** step on its own page — that is the one later team members will reuse.

### SOP 12 — SEO

SOP 30 is mentioned four times in about forty words on a single page. SOP 12 and SOP 30 are basically the same job; both spend steps telling you which of them to use. **Consolidate into one SOP.**

Broken Done when: “Confirm type the seed into Google and YouTube search.”

### SOP 13 — Updating the Marketing Website

No feedback.

### SOP 14 — Third-Party News and PR

No feedback.

### SOP 15 — Creator Prospecting

“Use SOP 9 for authors instead of this database” is a whole page whose only content is pointing somewhere else in the platform. Other SOPs have the same pointer-page pattern.

### SOP 16 — Creator Outreach

Pages built around one specific creator: “Link Amy Wang’s YouTube as @wamyy5” as a permanent step. Clearly meant as an example of how to do it; as written it reads as an instruction.

### SOP 17 — Creator Collab Calls

**Strongest content.** The unpaid-offer page handles an awkward conversation and keeps the legal guardrail next to it. Preserve that.

The SOP **branches** (paying vs not) but is laid out as a straight top-to-bottom list. “Continue to section 4 if they collab without a fee” is navigation dressed up as a task.

### SOP 19 — Lifecycle Emails and Tickets

### SOP 20 — Time Log

Needs to be more careful about personal / employee matters.

**Move up:** Done when currently says hours get used for planning or payroll. Missing the Friday form has pay consequences. That belongs at the **top of the page**, not the bottom.

This is **two SOPs in one**, mixed together:

1. Whoever fills the Tally form
2. Whoever follows up after team members

Split them.

### SOP 21 — Analytics

Four stacked callout boxes before a single step. The last one just says this is not the SOP you want.

Broken Done when: “Confirm clarify the decision and the deadline.”

### SOP 22 — Getting Set Up with Kahana Code

First place rules were treated as tasks. “Do not use kahana-web for kahana.io marketing” has a checkbox, a 5-minute estimate, and a Done when repeating it. Remove the checkbox, or Done when = the team member has read it.

### SOP 23 — Penetration Testing

No feedback.

### SOP 24 — PII Handling

More than half the SOP is standing rules with checkboxes. Feels like compliance theater: someone ticks eight boxes and thinks PII is handled. Pull every “do not” into a rules block at the top; leave only actual expected actions in the list.

(Later line says “SOP 23, 24, 25 — no feedback.” The notes above are kept; 23 and 25 are the ones with nothing else.)

### SOP 26 — Content Moderation

Step “record the decision code” never lists the decision codes. 20-minute estimate, no options. Reason codes **are** listed (adult, graphic violence, hate/harassment, spam, copyright, other) — that is why something was reported, not what the team member **decided**.
